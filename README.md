# Experiments — two extrapolation axes in many-body systems

Experiment archive for the paper

> **Two extrapolation axes decide whether interaction needs a module in many-body systems**

This repository contains the **code, logs and result tables only** — the manuscript and its
supplementary materials are not included.

## What the experiments are about

A many-body system is a group of components combined at some composition, whose property
belongs to the group rather than to any component alone. Chemical mixtures are its canonical
instance. Two ways of modelling them are compared:

- **v(ex)** — *explicit interaction*: all atoms of a mixture are placed in one graph and a
  transformer attends across molecules; interaction is modelled by a dedicated module.
- **v(im)** — *implicit interaction*: each component is encoded on its own, the resulting
  vectors are combined by a composition-weighted sum, and a small readout consumes the sum;
  no operation ever sees two components at once.

The central claim is that the two routes **fail on opposite axes** of extrapolation. When
test mixtures contain more components than any training mixture, v(ex) degrades and v(im)
does not; when test mixtures introduce components never seen in training, the ordering
reverses.

## Naming

Two modelling routes appear throughout; earlier notes used several labels for the same two
things. They are unified as **v(im)** and **v(ex)**:

| Route | Definition | Labels still present in file names and data columns |
|---|---|---|
| **v(im)** | per-molecule encoder → composition-weighted sum → readout | `e2`, `v(p)`, `per_molecule/` |
| **v(ex)** | all atoms in one graph → cross-molecule attention → readout | `e1`, `v(w)`, `whole_mixture/`, `vex_*` |

Result and log files keep their original names and internal `route` labels so that archived
numbers stay traceable. RDKit2D+XGBoost and the additive baseline are **external
references**, not routes.

## Environment

- Python 3.10+, PyTorch, PyTorch Geometric, RDKit, scikit-learn, XGBoost, pandas, scipy.
- All models are small (≈1.2 M parameters), pretraining-free, and trainable on a single
  consumer GPU.

## Data

- Chemical mixture data come from the public **CheMixHub** benchmark
  ([arXiv:2506.12231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12231)); scripts take `--data-base` /
  `--data-root` and are not vendored with the data.
- Portfolio price series are downloaded by `crossdomain/run_portfolio_vol.py` at run time.
- `results/multidoc_emb_20ng.npz` (68 MB, frozen BGE-M3 document embeddings for the
  20 Newsgroups experiment) is **not** included; it is regenerated by
  `scripts/crossdomain/run_multidoc_set.py`.
